\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Motivation and Problem Statement}

Large Language Models are increasingly capable of generating fluent, coherent, and contextually appropriate text at scale. While this progress enables a wide range of beneficial applications, it also raises significant concerns regarding the provenance and authenticity of generated content. In academic, journalistic, and online community contexts, it is often crucial to determine whether a piece of text was authored by a human or produced by an automated system. The inability to reliably make this distinction can lead to challenges in academic integrity, plagiarism detection, copyright enforcement, and the propagation of misinformation. It also makes further training of new models on AI-generated content more likely as more content online is AI-generated, which can lead to model degradation over time.

Several platforms have already introduced explicit policies governing the use of AI-generated content~\cite{so-gen-ai-policy}. However, policy-based approaches alone are difficult to enforce, as generated text can easily be copied, edited, or redistributed without attribution. This creates a technical problem: how can the origin of text generated by a language model be verified once it has left the control of the original provider?

Text watermarking offers a potential solution to this problem. By embedding a hidden statistical signal into generated text at generation time, it becomes possible to later test whether a given text was likely produced by a specific language model using a particular secret key. Ideally, such a watermark should be invisible to users, robust against common forms of text modification, and reliably detectable with a well-defined statistical guarantee.

Despite recent progress in this area, many watermarking approaches remain either theoretical or limited to offline experiments. Bridging the gap between theory and practical deployment remains an open challenge, especially when considering performance constraints, real-time generation, and interactive user interfaces.

\subsection{Project Goals and Scope}

The goal of this practical work is to design and implement a \emph{practical, end-to-end demonstration} of a modern text watermarking scheme for language models. The implementation is inspired primarily by the work of Kuditipudi et al.~\cite{kuditipudi2023robustarticle}, who propose a robust, distortion-free watermarking method based on Exponential-Minimum (Exp-Min) sampling.

Building on earlier seminar work, which focused on a Jupyter notebook-based proof of concept, this project extends the ideas into a deployable system consisting of:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A Python FastAPI backend responsible for text generation, watermark embedding, watermark detection, and controlled text corruption.
    \item A React-based frontend that allows users to interactively generate text, apply corruption strategies, and observe detection results in real time in a user-friendly manner.
    \item A streamlined watermarking and detection core that preserves the theoretical foundations of the original method while introducing selected modifications to improve runtime performance, usability for live demonstrations and deployment.
\end{itemize}

The scope of the project is intentionally practical rather than purely theoretical. While the watermarking algorithms are grounded in existing research, the focus lies on system design, API structure, and real-world constraints such as latency, streaming generation, and visualization of detection results. This project should not act as a one-to-one reproduction of the original paper, where deviations are introduced, these changes are explicitly discussed and justified.
